Electronic Timing
Circuits

01 · Everyday timers

Timers are everywhere

A timing circuit is a circuit that knows how long to wait.

It blinks, counts down, or keeps a steady beat.

every one of these has one inside
02 · Why keep time

Signals arrive at different times

Some wires are longer. Some parts are slower.

Read too early and you get a mix of old and new.

A clock says: read now

A clock is a shared beat, like a drummer in a band.

Everyone waits for the tick, then reads together.

03 · Timing with a bucket

A bucket and a narrow pipe

A capacitor stores electricity, like a bucket stores water. A resistor slows the flow, like a narrow pipe.

Filling takes time. That is how a circuit measures time.

Bigger bucket, slower fill

A bigger bucket, or a narrower pipe, takes longer to fill.

Pick the parts, and you pick the time.

04 · The 555 timer

Meet the 555

A cheap and famous timer chip. Billions have been made.

It fills the bucket, then drains it, then fills it again. Fill, drain, repeat.

the light is on while it fills

Keep only on and off

Forget the curve. Keep only the light: on is 1, off is 0.

Each jump up is a tick. That is a clock.

05 · Inside the 555

Inside the chip

Two watchers keep an eye on the bucket.

One asks: is it almost full? The other asks: is it almost empty?

They press two buttons

“Full!” presses RESET: open the drain. “Empty!” presses SET: close it.

A tiny memory holds the choice in between. It is called an SR latch.

let’s open it up
06 · The SR latch, step by step
meet the loop

Two gates, one loop

Each gate has one rule: its output is on only when both inputs are off.

The gates feed each other. The top light, Q, is our memory. Right now it is off.

step 1

Press SET

SET turns on an input of the bottom gate, so the bottom gate turns off.

Now the top gate sees two offs. Q turns on.

step 2

Let go

SET turns off, but Q stays on.

Q keeps the bottom gate off, so the loop holds itself. That is memory.

step 3

Press RESET

RESET turns on an input of the top gate, so Q turns off.

That lets the bottom gate turn back on.

step 4

Let go again

RESET turns off. Q stays off.

The latch remembers off too.

step 5

Never press both

Both gates turn off, so both lights are off. They should always be opposites.

Let go of both at once and it is a coin toss. Circuits avoid it.

that’s it

The whole trick

  • SETturns Q on
  • RESETturns Q off
  • neitherQ remembers
  • bothnever do this
07 · Back inside the 555

Now the loop makes sense

Fill until almost full, then RESET: drain.

Drain until almost empty, then SET: fill.

The latch remembers which way we are going. That is the beat.

08 · Three ways to use it
way 1

One-shot

Press once. The light stays on for a set time, then turns off by itself.

Like a stairway light or a hand dryer.

way 2

Blinker

Fill, drain, repeat, forever. A steady beat.

Like a turn signal or a bike light.

way 3

On / off

Use just the latch. SET turns it on, RESET turns it off.

Like the start and stop buttons on a machine.

09 · Quartz

A steadier beat

Buckets change with heat and age, so their timing drifts.

A tiny quartz crystal shakes at a very steady speed when you give it power.

your watch has one inside

Slow it down

The crystal shakes 32,768 times a second. Far too fast for a watch.

A chain of small circuits slows it down, step by step, to one tick per second.

10 · Inside a computer

Billions of ticks a second

A laptop’s clock ticks about 3 billion times every second.

One tick is so short that light only travels about a hand’s width.

The flip-flop

A flip-flop is a latch that only listens on the tick.

Like a camera: one snapshot per tick. Every part saves at the same moment.
